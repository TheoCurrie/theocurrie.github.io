01 — Wheelchair Assistive Device

SECOND-YEAR PROJECT

This individual project involved developing the mechanical locking and adjustment mechanism for a wheelchair-mounted assistive device. A focused product design specification was created covering structural, usability and manufacturing requirements.

Several design concepts were assessed before progressing the selected clamp and gear locking mechanism into CAD and prototype development. FMEA was used to identify failure risks including gear slip, clamp movement and axle loading, which informed subsequent design changes.

The final mechanism was validated using Autodesk Fusion simulations under the specified 140 N design load alongside physical testing of the operating force and prototype function.

02 — Cantilever Beam Vibration Analysis

SECOND-YEAR PROJECT

Experimental vibration testing was carried out on a cantilever beam using accelerometer and force-transducer measurements. MATLAB and LabVIEW were used to analyse the dynamic response of the system.

The investigation included decay testing, broadband excitation and stepped-sine testing. Experimental results were used to identify natural frequencies, damping characteristics and vibration mode behaviour.

03 — Power Systems & Motor Control

SECOND-YEAR PROJECT

MATLAB and Simulink were used to model and analyse AC and DC electrical circuits, allowing numerical calculations to be compared directly with simulated system behaviour.

A Permanent Magnet Synchronous Motor was also modelled using Direct Torque Control. The effect of parameter changes on system performance was assessed through speed, torque, flux linkage, overshoot and settling behaviour.

04 — IMechE Design Challenge

FIRST-YEAR PROJECT

Completed during the first year of my Mechanical Engineering degree, this team project involved designing, manufacturing and testing a mechanical device for the IMechE Design Challenge.

The project provided early practical experience in Autodesk Fusion 360, mechanical design, component selection and manufacturing. The design was developed through prototype testing and modification to improve the reliability and performance of the final device.

Working as part of the engineering team also developed my experience of collaborative design, translating concepts into manufacturable components and making practical engineering decisions under project constraints.

Personal Project 01 — Thermal Analysis Tool

INDEPENDENT ENGINEERING PROJECT · 2026
MATLAB · App Designer · Heat Transfer · Thermal Resistance · Lumped Capacitance · Biot Number · Model Validation

I developed this MATLAB App Designer tool independently to apply heat-transfer theory in an interactive engineering application. Users can select engineering materials from an external material-property database and define wall geometry, fluid temperatures and convection coefficients.

The tool performs steady-state thermal-resistance calculations incorporating conduction through a plane wall and convection at both surfaces. It calculates heat flux and the temperature distribution through the material and presents the results graphically.

A transient thermal model was also implemented using the lumped-capacitance method. The application calculates the material's transient temperature response and estimates the time required to reach within 1 °C of its equilibrium temperature.

Model validity is assessed using the Biot number. A low-Biot aluminium test case was checked independently using analytical calculations, with the application producing a steady-state heat flux of approximately 666.33 W/m² and a transient time of approximately 28.2 minutes to within 1 °C of equilibrium.

A deliberately high-Biot benchmark case was also evaluated. The application returned a Biot number of 2.0 and correctly identified that the lumped transient model was outside its validity range. This validation process was used to verify both the calculations and the limitations communicated by the application.
